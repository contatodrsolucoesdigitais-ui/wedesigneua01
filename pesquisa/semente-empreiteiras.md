## 2. The market and the moment

- **Region:** Mississippi, especially the Gulf Coast (Gulfport, Biloxi, Pascagoula), Jackson metro (Jackson,
  Madison, Brandon, Flowood), Hattiesburg, Tupelo, Meridian, Southaven/DeSoto County. Mostly small towns and rural
  service areas; the poorest US state by median household income (about $55k, approx.).
- **Who buys:** owner-operators of small trade businesses — roofing, remodeling, custom homes, concrete, HVAC,
  plumbing, electrical, fencing, dirt work. Typical crew of 2 to 15 people.
- **Seasonality:** hurricane season runs June to November; storm damage brings a burst of roofing and repair work
  and also a burst of out-of-state "storm chaser" crews. Local contractors resent storm chasers and lean on
  "local, licensed, been here 20 years" as their pitch. Winter is slower for exterior trades. Spring is busy.
- **Moment:** October 2026, end of hurricane season, contractors planning for a slower winter.
- **Licensing:** Mississippi requires a state license for residential work above certain thresholds; licenses and
  insurance are a trust signal homeowners ask about.
- **How homeowners find a contractor today:** a referral from a neighbor or church, then a Google search of the
  name; or "roof repair near me" on Google Maps; or a post in a local Facebook group ("anybody know a good roofer?").
- **Fact the seller leans on:** nearly half of actions on a Google Business listing go through the website link;
  a listing without a link loses that click even with better reviews.

## 3. The audience — five real-world profiles

### Profile A — Darrell, the old-school roofer
- 54, owns a roofing company in Gulfport. 6-man crew, about $900k a year in revenue *(approx.)*.
- 90% of work is word of mouth and insurance storm work. Has a Facebook page his daughter set up in 2017.
- Was burned before: paid a local guy $2,500 for a website in 2019; the guy vanished, the domain expired,
  now someone else owns it.
- Pains: storm chasers undercutting him after every hurricane; younger homeowners "don't call, they google".
- Objections: "Word of mouth has always worked." "Another monthly bill." "How do I know you won't vanish too?"
  "Where are you, exactly? I like to shake hands."
- Online: Facebook (local groups, buy/sell), YouTube (equipment), rarely Instagram. Answers the phone, hates email.

### Profile B — Marcus, the growing remodeler
- 37, kitchen and bath remodeler in Madison/Ridgeland. 4 employees plus subs. About $600k revenue *(approx.)*.
- Pays Angi and Thumbtack for leads; spends $800–1,500 a month on them and complains the leads are shared with
  three other contractors and half are tire-kickers.
- Has a Wix site he built himself in a weekend; it looks dated and he never updates it.
- Pains: wants higher-end jobs, wants to stop paying per lead. Too busy to touch the site.
- Objections: "I already have a site." "Will this actually bring calls or is it just pretty?" "What does it cost vs. Angi?"
- Online: Instagram (before/after photos), Facebook, Google Maps, some TikTok.

### Profile C — Linda, the wife who runs the office
- 49, does the books, scheduling and phone for her husband's HVAC company in Hattiesburg (8 techs).
- She is the real decision-maker for anything with a monthly charge. Reads every contract.
- Pains: the Google listing has wrong hours; customers call asking "do you service my town?".
- Objections: "What's the total cost per year?" "Is there a contract? Can we cancel?" "Who owns it if we leave?"
  "We got scammed by a 'Google listing verification' robocall company before."
- Online: Facebook, email, Nextdoor, local church and Chamber of Commerce groups.

### Profile D — Tyler, the new owner
- 29, started a concrete and fencing business near Southaven two years ago. 2 helpers. About $180k revenue *(approx.)*.
- Gets work from Facebook Marketplace posts and a Google listing with 23 reviews.
- Price-sensitive; every dollar counts. Comfortable with tech; could build a site on GoDaddy himself "someday".
- Pains: looks small next to established competitors; wants to look legit to bid bigger jobs.
- Objections: "I can do this myself for $15 a month." "I can't afford another bill in winter."
- Online: TikTok, Instagram, Facebook Marketplace, YouTube tutorials.

### Profile E — Mr. Puckett-type, the established commercial GC
- 62, second-generation commercial general contractor, 40+ employees, multi-state.
- Has a website, but it's 8 years old and managed by a nephew. Wants it to reflect the company's size.
- Pains: bidding on public and commercial work, where the website is checked by owners and architects.
- Objections: "Is a small agency serious enough for us?" "We need our project portfolio, safety record, bonding info."
- Online: LinkedIn, industry associations (AGC Mississippi), email.
