## 2. The market and the moment

- **Region:** Mississippi — Jackson metro (Jackson, Madison, Brandon, Pearl), Gulf Coast, Hattiesburg, Tupelo,
  Meridian, Southaven, plus many rural towns where the shop is the only one for 20 miles.
- **Who buys:** independent shop owners — general repair, tire shops, transmission, diesel, European specialists.
  1 to 8 bays. Many are second-generation family businesses.
- **Seasonality:** summer = A/C repairs and overheating; winter = batteries and tires; February–April = tax refund
  season, when customers finally fix what they postponed. Shops get slow in late fall and in January.
- **Moment:** October 2026, shops heading into the slower months.
- **Industry pressure:** dealers pushing service plans, newer cars under warranty going to dealers, national chains
  (Firestone, Midas, Take 5, Valvoline, Christian Brothers) opening in growing towns, technician shortage.
- **How drivers find a shop today:** "mechanic near me" on Google Maps while stuck, a Facebook group question,
  a friend's recommendation followed by a Google search of the shop's name.
- **Fact the seller leans on:** nearly half of actions on a Google listing go through the website link.

## 3. The audience — five real-world profiles

### Profile A — Ray, the 30-year independent
- 61, general repair shop in Hattiesburg, 4 bays, 3 techs. About $700k revenue *(approx.)*.
- Bays are full most weeks from regulars who've come for 20+ years. Google listing has 94 reviews, 4.8 stars, no website.
- Pains: regulars aging out, can't find techs, younger customers don't know he exists.
- Objections: "My bays are full." "I don't need more customers, I need more techs." "Another salesman."
- Online: barely. Facebook occasionally. The phone at the counter is his CRM.

### Profile B — Jessica, the second-generation owner
- 34, took over her father's tire and service shop in Tupelo two years ago.
- Wants to modernize: online booking, text reminders, a real brand. Already uses shop management software
  (Tekmetric/Shopmonkey-type) that offers a basic website add-on.
- Pains: dad's old customer base shrinking; national chains opened down the road.
- Objections: "My shop software already gives me a website." "Can it connect to my booking?" "What's the ROI?"
- Online: Instagram, Facebook, Google reviews (replies to every one), industry Facebook groups for shop owners.

### Profile C — Luis, the specialist
- 45, European import specialist (BMW, Mercedes, Audi) in the Jackson metro. 3 bays, high ticket, $2k average repair *(approx.)*.
- Wants the right customers, not more customers: owners of out-of-warranty German cars avoiding dealer prices.
- Pains: dealer charges more, but customers assume dealer = better; he needs to look as premium as the dealer.
- Objections: "A generic site makes me look like a cheap shop." "Will it rank for 'BMW repair Jackson'?"
- Online: Google, brand-specific forums, Instagram.

### Profile D — Bubba, the rural one-man shop
- 50, one-bay shop plus roadside work in a town of 3,000 near Meridian.
- Cash-based, tight margins. Customers are everybody in town.
- Pains: highway traffic and newcomers don't know he exists; he loses tow-ins to the next town.
- Objections: "Everybody here knows me." "I can't spend $100+ a month on something I can't see."
- Online: Facebook, mostly through his wife's account.

### Profile E — Denise, the office manager at a 6-bay shop
- 42, runs front desk, billing and marketing for a busy shop in Southaven (Memphis suburbs).
- Has been cold-called by "Google listing" scammers and SEO agencies for years. Pays $400/month to a
  directory/marketing company on a 12-month contract she regrets.
- Objections: "We're locked into a contract already." "Every agency promises calls." "Show me numbers from a real shop."
- Online: Facebook, email, Nextdoor, local business groups.
